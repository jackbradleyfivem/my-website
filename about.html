About

A family kitchen that learned how to feed a crowd.

Since April 2015, Abanguni Hospitality Consultancy has grown from a small family business into a specialist banqueting, outdoor and private catering company in Johannesburg.

The company wants the Abanguni name to be a wholly Black-owned business that is taken seriously in entertainment and catering, in the region and across South Africa. The practical aim is to be a preferred supplier of event management, catering and the work that sits beside it.

With partners, they want to be part of urban regeneration in the inner city and the townships, and to hire people from the communities where the work happens. The published line is simple: a responsible member of society, involved in community projects.

Day to day, that shows up as hospitality shaped around the client. Quality is treated as a people problem: training, development, and paying attention to how the work is done.

Talk about an event

Mission

A gathering people can enjoy.

To provide hospitality tailored to the client, so they get a pleasant room and a gathering that works. Quality comes through the team, by investing in training and keeping up with how the work is done well.

Vision

Trusted, and properly led.

To be a hospitality firm people can rely on, driven by competent leadership and staff. The longer aim is a leading supplier of event management, catering and associated services, nationally and beyond.

Where the work goes

Based in Bertrams, serving Johannesburg, the rest of the country, and neighbouring countries.

On the ground

  • Drop-off, small and large indoor events, outdoor events, and complex event management
  • Weddings, birthdays, corporate functions, cocktails, bachelor parties, funerals and private family functions
  • Décor, and function staff: bartenders, setup, service and cleaning

Kitchens

  • A mobile kitchen in caravans for sports events and other outdoor functions
  • A fully functioning kitchen for ready-cooked meals, takeaway and sit-down
  • Signature plates include slow lamb stew, a whole roasted leg, and curries with what they call an African twist
